Sale and structured valuation of residential and commercial properties

AValuation

Substance and capitalised value in focus

The value of an apartment building is not decided by its address alone, but by the tenant structure, the gap between contractual rent and the local rent index, and the actual state of repair.

A positive rent gap, meaning headroom between the current rent and the achievable market rent, is often the decisive value driver, yet standard estimates rarely work it out properly.

The requirements of the Building Energy Act (GEG) increasingly enter the valuation as well: energy performance and pending refurbishment affect both the achievable price and the circle of solvent buyers.

The valuation itself follows the income approach or a DCF method, with the property yield rate as the central lever for capitalising future income.

The off-market approach

CTerms used in valuation
Income approach (Ertragswertverfahren)
The method used to determine the market value of income producing property. It derives value from sustainably achievable income rather than from construction cost. Its legal basis is the German property valuation ordinance, ImmoWertV.
Gross and net income
Gross income is the annual rent before deductions. Subtracting operating costs, meaning management, maintenance, rent default risk and non-recoverable running costs, gives the net income.
Property yield rate (Liegenschaftszinssatz)
The rate at which a property’s future income is discounted to present value. Local valuation boards derive it from purchase prices actually recorded. It differs by location and property type.
Rent gap (Mietspiegel-Delta)
The distance between the rent currently agreed and the local comparative rent. A positive gap describes legally permissible headroom for increases and feeds directly into the capitalised value.
Building Energy Act (GEG)
The act governs energy requirements for buildings. For existing properties it determines which measures may fall due after a change of ownership, and thereby affects both the achievable price and the circle of possible buyers.
FAQFrequently asked questions

How is the value of a multi-family house in the Rhineland determined?

Valuation is usually based on the income approach or a DCF method, where the property yield rate converts future income into a present value. Tenant structure, the rent gap and the energy performance measured against GEG standards feed in as well and often change the result more than the land value alone.
