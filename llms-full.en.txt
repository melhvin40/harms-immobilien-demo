### Substance and capitalised value in focus

The value of an apartment building is not decided by its address alone, but by the tenant structure, the gap between contractual rent and the local rent index, and the actual state of repair.

A positive rent gap, meaning headroom between the current rent and the achievable market rent, is often the decisive value driver, yet standard estimates rarely work it out properly.

The requirements of the Building Energy Act (GEG) increasingly enter the valuation as well: energy performance and pending refurbishment affect both the achievable price and the circle of solvent buyers.

The valuation itself follows the income approach or a DCF method, with the property yield rate as the central lever for capitalising future income.

### The off-market approach

An apartment building listed in public unsettles tenants before the sale is even concluded, and it signals to the market a need to sell that weakens the negotiating position.

Vetted investor networks, grown over more than three decades, replace the reach of a portal with precision: the property reaches only buyers who already meet the criteria on creditworthiness, investment horizon and property profile, without a single prospect being able to connect the property publicly with its owner.

## Erbbaurecht und Sondersituationen

### The heritable building right as a discipline of its own

A heritable building right (Erbbaurecht) separates land value and building value consistently. The buyer acquires the building and a time limited right to use the land, not the land itself.

This separation becomes the decisive factor once the remaining term falls below 40 years: banks increasingly restrict lending, which narrows the circle of buyers and, without a well founded counter calculation, leads to noticeable price discounts.

A serious valuation sets land value, building value and remaining term properly against one another and thereby shows where an apparent loss in value is in fact negotiable.

### Legal framework and section 9a ErbbauRG

Where the building on a heritable building right serves residential purposes, section 9a ErbbauRG protects the leaseholder against inequitable increases in the ground rent: an adjustment is permissible at the earliest every three years and may not exceed the change in general economic conditions since the contract was concluded, in particular the cost of living.

For residential heritable building rights, increases in land value alone do not in principle justify an increase.

Whether an adjustment demanded by the landowner withstands this fairness test is decided by the correct value protection clause and the applicable index series, a point at which negotiating confidence towards foundations, municipalities and institutional landowners really shows.

### Special situations

- Sale of heritable building rights with an expiring remaining term
- Negotiation of ground rent adjustments with church, municipal and foundation owned landowners
- Non-performing portfolios on behalf of credit institutions and insolvency administrators
- Communities of heirs with diverging interests in selling within one portfolio

Heritable building rights do not forgive standard solutions. An initial conversation clarifies whether your ground rent adjustment withstands the fairness test of section 9a ErbbauRG at all.

## Frequently asked questions

### How is the value of a multi-family house in the Rhineland determined?

Valuation is usually based on the income approach or a DCF method, where the property yield rate converts future income into a present value. Tenant structure, the rent gap and the energy performance measured against GEG standards feed in as well and often change the result more than the land value alone.

### Can a property on land held under a heritable building right be sold before the term ends?

Yes. A heritable building right is building ownership combined with a right of use and can be transferred independently, regardless of the remaining term. As the remaining term shortens, however, banks restrict lending limits, so that negotiating an extension with the landowner often influences the achievable price more markedly than the timing of the sale itself.

### When may the landowner increase the ground rent?

For heritable building rights serving residential purposes, the fairness test under section 9a ErbbauRG applies: an increase is permissible at the earliest after three years and may not exceed the change in general economic conditions since the contract was concluded, in particular the consumer price index. Increases in land value alone do not justify an adjustment.

### What are the advantages of an off-market sale of residential and commercial properties?

Off-market protects the owner’s reputation, because neither tenants nor their professional circle learn of the sale before it is concluded. It also avoids the unrest of a publicly visible sales process and, through buyers whose creditworthiness has been checked in advance, creates greater certainty of completion.
